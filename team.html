I like to think of myself as a behavior detective.

Courtney St. Germain, OTR/L

Founder & Clinical Director | DIR-Expert Practitioner

Courtney is the founder and Clinical Director of Child & Family Development and a licensed Occupational Therapist with almost 15 years of experience supporting children and young adults. She earned her Master of Science in Occupational Therapy from Brenau University and became a certified DIR-Expert Practitioner in 2019. Licensed in New Jersey, New York, and Florida, she has advanced training in sensory integration, feeding therapy, and aquatic therapy. A former ICDL DIR Training Leader, Courtney also provides professional mentorship and consultation to therapists locally and internationally.

Her clinical experience spans early intervention, school-based services, home-based therapy, and private practice, supporting children with autism, ADHD, sensory processing differences, developmental delays, and emotional regulation challenges. She is especially passionate about neuroscience and understanding how relationships, sensory processing, and motor development work together to shape a child's development and everyday experiences.

Courtney describes herself as a "behavior detective." Rather than focusing solely on what a child is doing, she seeks to understand why, recognizing behavior as meaningful communication. She believes therapy begins with safety, regulation, and genuine connection, creating opportunities for children to explore, problem-solve, and develop confidence in their abilities. For Courtney, some of the most meaningful moments are the seemingly small victories that reflect profound developmental growth, especially when families begin to see and understand their child's world in a new way.

I believe children thrive when they feel loved and understood.

Occupational therapy, education and DIR/Floortime staff, across the preschool, the day program and individual sessions.

Sarah Hewitt, BA

Center Operations & Curriculum Lead | DIR/Floortime Assistant

Sarah is the Center Operations and Curriculum Lead at Child & Family Development and a certified DIR/Floortime Assistant with 15 years of experience in early childhood education and childcare. She earned her Bachelor of Arts in English from William Paterson University and obtained her DIR/Floortime Assistant Certification in 2026. Her extensive background includes supporting children from infancy through age twelve across a variety of developmental and educational settings.

Her experience includes working with children with autism, ADHD, developmental delays, sensory processing differences, and communication and learning differences. Having spent much of her career supporting emerging communicators, Sarah is especially passionate about language development and creating environments where children feel encouraged to explore new sounds, words, and ways of expressing themselves.

Sarah believes that development is not a straight line, but a journey filled with discoveries, challenges, and meaningful victories. She recognizes that every child learns and grows differently and that progress should be measured by each child's individual development rather than societal expectations. Through her work in both curriculum development and direct support, she strives to create engaging, language-rich environments where children feel safe to experiment, discover their interests, and develop confidence in their abilities. For Sarah, the goal is not simply helping children reach milestones, but giving them the freedom and encouragement to discover who they are.

I believe children thrive when they are given the space, support, and encouragement to discover who they are and what they like.

Alicia Bergen, COTA/L

Certified Occupational Therapy Assistant | DIR Basic Certificate

Alicia is a licensed Certified Occupational Therapy Assistant with nearly 11 years of pediatric experience in clinical and school-based settings. She holds a Bachelor's degree from William Paterson University and an Associate's degree in Occupational Therapy from Eastwick College. Licensed in New Jersey and New York, Alicia has completed DIR 101 and DIR 201 through ICDL and is a Basic Floortime Practitioner.

Her clinical experience includes supporting children and young adults with autism, ADHD, developmental delays, sensory processing differences, motor planning challenges, and emotional regulation needs. She is especially passionate about DIR/Floortime, sensory processing, executive functioning, and early childhood development.

Alicia believes meaningful growth begins with trust and connection. As a proud mom to her neurodivergent son, Oliver, she brings both professional expertise and personal understanding to her work. Oliver has deepened her appreciation for celebrating each child's individuality, honoring their unique perspective, and recognizing the importance of meeting children where they are. She strives to create playful, supportive experiences where children feel understood, accepted, and empowered to grow at their own pace.

I believe children thrive when they feel safe, understood, and when they are given the time and space to grow at their own pace.

Elizabeth (Liz) Yockey, MA

Homeschool Support | Educator | DIR Basic Certificate

Liz is an experienced educator and DIR/Floortime Practitioner with over 20 years of experience supporting children across a variety of educational and developmental settings. She holds a Master of Arts from New York University's Steinhardt School of Culture, Education, and Human Development and a DIR Floortime BASIC Certificate. Her background includes teaching math and science, developmental preschool education, curriculum design, homeschooling, and outdoor education.

Liz is especially passionate about supporting neurodivergent children and those whose unique learning styles may not fit traditional educational expectations. As an autistic, twice-exceptional individual and parent, she brings both professional experience and personal perspective to her work. She believes everyone possesses their own combination of strengths and challenges, and that meaningful learning begins with recognizing and embracing those differences.

Liz's approach is rooted in curiosity, observation, and flexibility. Rather than making assumptions based on a diagnosis, she takes time to understand each child's individual needs, interests, and ways of communicating. She also recognizes that development is not linear. A child's abilities may look different from one day to the next. By creating opportunities for play, exploration, and authentic connection, Liz helps children build confidence, discover their strengths, and participate meaningfully in their communities.

I believe children thrive when they have as much freedom to play as we can allow them while being in community with others.

Mya Huerta

DIR/Floortime Assistant

Mya is a DIR/Floortime Assistant with a Bachelor's degree in Psychology and experience supporting children across developmental, educational, and recreational settings. Her background includes working as a Montessori Teacher Assistant and youth sports coach, where she developed a strong appreciation for the importance of play, movement, and meaningful relationships in supporting children's growth.

Her experience includes working with children with autism, sensory processing differences, developmental delays, communication challenges, and emotional regulation needs. She is especially passionate about DIR/Floortime, sensory processing, and social-emotional development, with a particular interest in helping children build confidence, communication skills, and independence through playful, relationship-based experiences.

Mya believes that meaningful growth begins with connection, not expectations. She takes time to understand each child's unique interests, strengths, and ways of experiencing the world, allowing those discoveries to guide her interactions. By following a child's lead and joining them in activities that bring joy and curiosity, she creates opportunities for children to explore, communicate, and develop new skills in an environment where they feel safe and supported. For Mya, the most important part of her work is helping every child feel comfortable and confident being exactly who they are.

I believe children thrive when they feel safe, understood, supported, and free to be themselves while exploring the world around them.

Ritaj Shoman, BSW

DIR/Floortime Assistant

Ritaj is a DIR/Floortime Assistant with a Bachelor of Social Work and foundational training in DIR/Floortime through ICDL's DIR 102 course. Her professional background includes supporting infants, toddlers, young children, and expectant mothers through home-based services, with a particular focus on families facing socioeconomic challenges.

Her experience includes working with infants as young as six weeks old and children with autism, developmental delays, communication differences, and those born prematurely. She is especially interested in sensory processing, speech and language development, emotional regulation, and motor planning, recognizing how these areas influence a child's ability to connect, communicate, and participate in everyday experiences.

Ritaj believes that every child deserves the freedom to explore, make mistakes, and grow in their own unique way. Her approach is rooted in patience, genuine connection, and understanding each child's individual strengths and needs. She finds joy in celebrating the small moments of progress that build confidence over time and values working alongside families as they discover new ways to support their child's development. For Ritaj, creating a safe, encouraging environment where children feel accepted is the foundation for meaningful learning and growth.

I believe children thrive when they feel valued, understood, and encouraged to be themselves.

Jillian Kirby, COTA/L

Certified Occupational Therapy Assistant | DIR/Floortime Assistant

Jillian is a licensed Certified Occupational Therapy Assistant with experience across pediatric and adult settings, including rehabilitation, hospital-based care, and school-based services. She holds an Associate of Science in Occupational Therapy and is currently pursuing her Master of Science in Occupational Therapy, expected in December 2026.

Her clinical experience includes supporting individuals with autism, developmental delays, sensory processing differences, and physical challenges. She has specialized experience in myofascial release, hippotherapy, and pediatric care, along with foundational training in DIR/Floortime through ICDL.

Jillian's approach to therapy is guided by a simple philosophy: "I like to eat dessert before the main course." Just as dessert brings joy before the meal, Jillian believes relationships should come before expectations. Inspired by the relationship-based foundation of DIR/Floortime, she prioritizes building trust, understanding each child's unique interests, and creating meaningful connections before introducing new challenges. She believes that when children feel genuinely seen, understood, and valued, they are better supported to explore, engage, and grow.

I believe children thrive when they are understood. Our role is not simply to teach children how to fit into our world, but to enter their world first so we can build something together.
